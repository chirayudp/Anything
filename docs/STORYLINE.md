# MoneyQuest: Simple & Student-Friendly Scenario Design (Class 11 & 12 Focus)

- No Standalone Single-Choice Questions: Every scenario now functions as an interactive 3-Decision Sequence.
- Deterministic Combined Scoring:
  - Each of the 3 steps yields a tier: BEST (3 pts), GOOD (2 pts), BAD (1 pt), or VERY_BAD (0 pts).
  - Total Score (out of 9):
    - 8-9 points: Overall BEST (City Cleared with Honors)
    - 6-7 points: Overall GOOD (City Passed with Feedback)
    - 3-5 points: Overall BAD (Review & Retry Prompted)
    - 0-2 points: Overall VERY_BAD (Recovery Mode Triggered)
- Progressive Consequence Flow: The choices in Step 1 carry forward into the money or resources available in Step 2 and Step 3.
- Simple Language & Idea Cards: Short sentences and student-friendly analogies without complex financial jargon.

## Pillar 1: Plan & Budget (PB)

### Idea Card: Needs vs Wants
- Need: Something you must have to live and study (like food, bus ticket, room rent, and school books).
- Want: Something nice to have that you can live without (like movie tickets, video games, and fancy snacks).

#### PB_001: Your First Scholarship Pocket Money
- Where you are: Your student hostel room.
- Who you meet: Anand, your friendly senior roommate.
- The Story: You just received ₹6,000 as your monthly student scholarship pocket money. The month has 30 days ahead of you.

Decision 1: Splitting the ₹6,000 Stipend (Sliders)
- What you must do: Move the sliders to divide your ₹6,000 between Important Needs, Fun Wants, and Emergency Money.
- Options & Results:
  1. BEST: ₹3,600 for Needs, ₹1,200 for Wants, and ₹1,200 for Emergency Money.
     - What happens: You eat healthy food, travel on time, and still have money saved for sudden troubles.
  2. GOOD: ₹4,000 for Needs, 1,500 for Wants, and ₹500 for Emergency Money.
     - What happens: All bills are paid, but your safety money is quite small.
  3. BAD: ₹3,000 for Needs, ₹2,800 for Wants, and 200 for Emergency Money.
     - What happens: You have fun in week one, but you run out of money for meals in week four.
  4. VERY BAD: Spending 5,500 on new shoes and video games right away.
     - What happens: You cannot pay for your hostel food and have no money left.

Decision 2: The Mid-Month Mess Bill Arrives
- What you must do: On Day 15, the hostel warden asks for ₹2,800 mess fees. Choose how to pay.
- Options & Results:
  1. BEST: Pay on time from your pre-allocated Needs envelope.
     - What happens: Your food account is cleared with zero debt or penalties.
  2. GOOD: Ask for a 3-day grace period to withdraw cash from your savings account.
     - What happens: The bill is paid, but you face minor administrative delays.
  3. BAD: Empty your emergency money because you spent your Needs cash on snacks.
     - What happens: Your food is covered, but any sudden surprise will now leave you stranded.
  4. VERY BAD: Borrow ₹2,800 from a high-interest mobile lending app.
     - What happens: You face high hidden interest charges and aggressive collection calls.

Decision 3: The Canteen Celebration Invite
- What you must do: On Day 25, friends ask you to join an end-of-month celebration costing ₹500. You have ₹300 left in your Wants pile.
- Options & Results:
  1. BEST: Order a 200 snack and stay within your remaining ₹300 Wants limit.
     - What happens: You celebrate with friends and finish the month inside your spending limit.
  2. GOOD: Skip the expensive food, join the table to chat, and eat your regular hostel meal later.
     - What happens: You socialize and spend zero money, keeping your ₹300 intact.
  3. BAD: Dip into your emergency safety envelope to buy a full ₹500 luxury meal.
     - What happens: You overspend on discretionary leisure by raiding your safety cushion.
  4. VERY BAD: Treat everyone on store credit to look generous.
     - What happens: You start next month in debt before your new stipend even arrives.

## Pillar 2: Save & Manage (SM)

### Idea Card: Emergency Money & Sinking Funds
- Emergency Money: Cash set aside only for sudden surprises (like a flat bicycle tire, a broken phone screen, or buying medicine when sick).
- Sinking Fund: Saving small amounts each week or month for a planned expense (like holiday train tickets or next term's exam fees), instead of paying a huge sum all at once.

## Pillar 3: Understand Investing (Simulations Only) (INV)

### Idea Card: The Mango Tree Story (Safe vs Risky Growth)
- Safe Savings (Fixed Growth): Like a sturdy lemon tree. It gives a few lemons every week without fail.
- Growing Investment (Variable Growth): Like a mango tree. In good years it gives lots of sweet fruit, but in bad weather it may give very few.
- Golden Rule: Only invest money that you will NOT need for daily life for at least 3 years. Keep your emergency cash completely safe!
